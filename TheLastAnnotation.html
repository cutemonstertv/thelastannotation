Chapter One

The Annotator

The kettle clicks off and I don't get up.

This is not unusual. The kettle clicks off six, seven times a day in this flat and I have developed a relationship with it that is closer to negotiation than use. I will go to it. I will. After this paragraph. After this sentence in the margin that I have been turning over since breakfast, trying to decide whether the man who wrote it meant cannot as in physically unable or cannot as in will not permit himself. There is a difference. There is always a difference, and I have spent three years in the difference.

The book is open on the kitchen table at page two hundred and fourteen. The spine has given up pretending it is a spine. The pages are the colour of weak tea. Someone — him — has written in the left margin in a hand so small it looks like it was trying not to be caught:

Grief is not the absence of the person. It is the presence of the space they occupied, which refuses to close.

I have underlined it twice. Once in pencil, the first year. Once in black ink, last winter, when I understood he was right and hated him a little for it.

Outside, Stoke Newington is doing its Tuesday. A bus sighs at the lights on Church Street. Someone in the flat below is practising scales on a keyboard that has one note slightly flat, always the same note, which I have come to think of as a kind of neighbour. Rain is considering the windows and deciding against them. The radiator under the sink ticks once, companionably, like a dog shifting in its sleep.

I cannot remember the last time I was cold.

This is not a complaint. The heating in this building is the one reliable thing about it — older than the laminate, older than the intercom that no one uses, older, I sometimes think, than the idea of comfort itself. The pipes knock in a language I have learned not to translate. I dress for weather I never quite feel.

I write in the margin, under his line, in the small cramped hand I have developed from answering him:

And what if the space starts answering back?

The pen is a cheap one. Blue. I cap it. I look up.

There is a man on the other side of the kitchen table.
